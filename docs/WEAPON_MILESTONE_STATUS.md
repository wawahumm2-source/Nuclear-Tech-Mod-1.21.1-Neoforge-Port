# Modern HBM Gun Framework: Milestone Status

Status date: 2026-08-11

This ledger separates implemented code from verified behavior. The first four-gun slice is structurally playable, but roster expansion remains blocked until the open manual and multiplayer gates are completed.

## Implemented Scope

- Four data-driven pilot guns: Target Pistol, StG 77, SPAS-12, and Congo Lake.
- Eight survival-craftable ammunition profiles: FMJ/AP pistol and rifle rounds, buckshot/slug shells, and HE/HEAT 40 mm grenades.
- Server-authoritative inputs, ammunition, fire timing, spread, damage, reload phases, fire modes, and stack identity.
- Versioned persistent gun state plus transient per-player weapon sessions.
- Lightweight swept trajectories for bullets and pellets; visible entities only for grenade-class projectiles.
- Headshots, partial armor penetration, fragile-block impacts, HBM explosion routing, movement weight, ADS, recoil, tracers, casings, smoke, flashes, screenshake, and HBM sound events.
- Atomic datapack definition reloads, reusable behavior registration, Curios ammo-provider integration, and a shared GeckoLib/OBJ renderer.
- Original-HBM-derived pilot geometry, textures, sounds, and animation translations with per-asset provenance.
- GPL-attributed Superb Warfare presentation architecture: synthetic camera/root hierarchy, eased ADS, continuous walk/sprint/draw motion, nonlinear recoil curves, player-skin arms attached to viewmodel-space hand bones, mechanism motion, and model-local muzzle flare.
- The first-person renderer cancels GeckoLib's conventional half-block item-centering offset for already-centered HBM OBJ coordinates. Muzzle flashes now use explicit HBM muzzle coordinates and bounded sizes rather than misusing a rotation pivot as a position.
- Superb-style dynamic segmented crosshair, four smooth server-confirmed hit states, and a bottom-right gun/ammunition panel rendered from HBM/vanilla primitives and item icons. Body hits use a white X, headshots add a smaller red inner plus, kills use a red X, and headshot kills combine the red X and inner plus; none use a center dot. Block impacts and rejected damage remain world particles only, so misses cannot create hit feedback and feedback cannot be client-claimed.
- HBM-authored presentation rigs for all four pilots. The failed Target Pistol profile and its near-camera world-space flash were removed. Its hip-fire and physical iron-sight ADS endpoints are now accepted and baked into the replacement rig; action-state visual acceptance remains open.
- The Target Pistol remains the reference weapon under active visual correction. Its handling baseline is Superb Warfare's MP-443, with locally responsive eased ADS/FOV and sensitivity, continuous walk/sway/sprint/draw motion, nonlinear recoil and camera response, player-skin arms, and distinct 2.25-second tactical and 2.65-second empty reload sequences. The HBM mesh, texture, sounds, and `.22 LR` ammunition identity remain unchanged.
- Follow-up playtesting exposed five Target Pistol defects: state-component changes invoked vanilla re-equip lowering after firing/ADS reconciliation, procedural motion was sampled at 20 Hz, the pistol was too distant and small, the arm anchors did not extend naturally from the screen, and ADS changed FOV without placing the physical sights on the camera axis. The state-reset and frame-interpolation defects are covered by automated tests. Repeated compile-time pose guesses were rejected because gun-axis, outer viewmodel, and player-arm transforms were coupled. The replacement hierarchy isolates HBM geometry on `model_space`, leaves both player arms outside that space, and applies Superb Warfare's GPL arm-rendering sequence without importing its protected model-resource coordinates. Hip fire and ADS now have distinct baked outer-rig, model-space, right-arm, and left-arm endpoints. One eased render-frame value interpolates every layer together. Calibration schema 4 rejects the former shared-pose file, preventing centered ADS geometry from overwriting the offset hip pose.
- The next Target Pistol correction isolates GUI and third-person transforms from first-person calibration. Opening chat or inventory now blocks input without resetting the held-stack presentation, eliminating the intermittent side-on fallback and 115-degree equip sweep. The GUI icon is enlarged, third person uses the Superb Warfare arm-pose contract, movement bob cadence is reduced from roughly four-to-five cycles per second to about one-to-1.4, and ADS cancels sprint on both client and server while fully suppressing residual movement bob.
- The current Target Pistol pass uses Superb Warfare's MP-443 third-person item translation and scale with the HBM mesh, suppresses procedural and vanilla view bob at full ADS, and gives reload priority over ADS/sprint. Hand rotations are authored as absolute hip/ADS endpoints rather than frame-accumulated GeckoLib deltas, eliminating the ADS arm spin, while the mechanism clips and smooth outer reload envelope preserve readable magazine action. Hip-fire shots remain centered on the visible reticle. ADS uses the physical front-sight axis at `0.65` degrees below screen center and the server solves a gravity-compensated `50`-block zero using the same tick simulation as live rounds. The gameplay-first `.22 LR` profile launches at `16` blocks per tick with `0.0245` gravity, `0.001` drag, and a `500`-block travel envelope: flat enough for ordinary Minecraft fights, but with readable long-range drop. A fire press during sprint now authoritatively drops to hipfire for a three-tick settle, fires one shot, remains raised for a four-tick recovery, and only then eases back into sprint while Ctrl and forward movement remain held. ADS remains available while Ctrl is held, prevents sprint resumption while active, and explicitly restores the still-held sprint intent after RMB is released. Off-hand guns render no first- or third-person model, select no gun arm pose, and cannot enter the main-hand-only gameplay service. Third-person muzzle flashes, smoke, and casing particles are suppressed; a centered over-the-shoulder reticle, remote reload arm pose, visible warm `.22` tracer segments, and smaller first-person casings are active. The existing `1.8x` headshot multiplier is covered by definition and live damage tests.

## Quick Load

Double-click `Quick Load Latest Build.bat` in the project root to launch an isolated comparison client containing the current HBM workspace build and the supplied Superb Warfare `0.8.9` reference at commit `9b5284f4`. `Quick View Latest Build.bat` remains as a backwards-compatible alias. Both use `tools/quick-view-latest-build.ps1`, which verifies Java 21 and the exact external Superb Warfare jar hash, stages that jar only under the ignored `run-client-comparison/mods` directory, and invokes `gradlew runClientComparison`. The comparison jar is never included in HBM source, normal test runs, or the packaged HBM jar.

Pass `-InfoOnly` from a terminal to verify the detected Java and latest jar without launching Minecraft. Pass `-RebuildFirst` to force a complete build before launch.

While holding the Target Pistol in first person, the temporary calibration HUD is visible by default. `F10` cycles MODEL / ADS POSE / RIGHT ARM / LEFT ARM groups, `F9` cycles fields within that group, and holding Left/Right adjusts the selected value. Shift uses a coarse step, Ctrl uses a fine step, Home resets all values, and `F8` hides or shows the overlay and colored anchors. Values autosave to `run-client-comparison/config/hbm-target-pistol-calibration.json`. The approved hip-fire and ADS values are baked into the HBM rig; retain the tool only through the remaining fire/reload/sprint transition inspection, then remove it before release.

## Open Release Gates

- The Target Pistol hip-fire and ADS endpoints are approved and baked. Inspect repeated ADS entry/exit, fire while hip/ADS, white body hits, white/red headshots, red kills, combined headshot kills, misses against air and blocks, the `50`-block ballistic zero and long-range drop, dry-fire, empty slide lock, sprint-fire settle/fire/resume, Ctrl-held ADS priority, wall lowering, chat/inventory open-close, off-hand suppression, real weapon switching, the MP-443-derived third-person transform and over-the-shoulder reticle, remote reload pose, visible `.22` tracer segments, absence of third-person muzzle/casing effects, smaller first-person casings, and both complete reloads. Confirm that the right arm never spins, full ADS has neither weapon nor camera bob, hip shots track the centered visible reticle, ADS rounds cross the front-sight axis near 50 blocks, confirmations sit on the sight zero, screens no longer trigger the side-on equip sweep, the enlarged GUI icon remains within its slot, movement is frame-smooth, both skin arms extend naturally from the screen edge, and the viewmodel does not clip. Remove the temporary calibration UI only after this action-state pass. Then perform the same checks for StG 77, SPAS-12, and Congo Lake plus dropped-form, FOV-extreme, and shader-loader checks. Automated transform tests cannot replace this visual gate.
- Run two-player tests at simulated 100-200 ms latency for automatic fire, reload cancellation, switching, death, reconnect, and dimension transfer.
- Expand live GameTests to cover reload/inventory transactions with a fully negotiated test client, death/drop/save/reload, chunk unloading, and inventory-full unloading. The pure state suite covers between-shell interruption; Minecraft's mock server player cannot negotiate Curios or HBM payloads and is not a valid substitute for this gate.
- Exercise malicious held-stack and impossible-timing payloads against a real connected client. Pure tests already cover stale sequence rejection and the eight-packets-per-tick limit, but they do not replace protocol-level abuse testing.
- Replace the Original HBM SPAS-12 fallback if a complete, authorized Neo Edition SPAS asset set is located. The supplied Neo archive contains no such set.
- Replace temporary steel-ingot and ammunition-stamp access recipes when the final HBM material and assembly-machine progression is active.
- Add final assembly-machine recipes before moving the four guns out of creative/test-only availability.

## Expansion Rule

Do not expand the firearm roster while any visual, multiplayer, or performance gate above remains open. Additional weapon families must arrive in batches of four to six and express exceptions through reusable `GunBehavior` or ammunition effects.
